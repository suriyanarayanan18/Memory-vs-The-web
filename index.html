04 The mechanism

How an AI decides which sites to use

There is no published algorithm. What follows is the working understanding from citation studies and from how AI search is built, and it lines up with the patterns in this data.

01
Retrieve.

The model runs one or more searches and pulls a pool of candidate pages. It is not reading the whole internet, only what those searches surface.

02
Score and cite.

A separate step ranks passages from that pool for relevance and trust, and only the strongest become citations. The gap between what was retrieved and what was cited is this scoring step made visible, and this study captured both.

Takeaway. The selection logic is opaque and differs by engine, which is exactly why a measurement project like this exists. You cannot read the rules, so you probe the outputs and infer the patterns.

06 Method

Method and limitations

  • Questions. 30 buyer-intent queries across five types: category, problem, use case, budget, comparison.
  • Memory run. The model answered with no web access, reflecting its training data.
  • Retrieval run. The same questions with web search on, capturing live citations.
  • Extraction. Brand mentions were pulled from each answer by a model; cited URLs were reduced to unique domains in code.
  • Share of voice. The percent of questions in a mode whose answer mentioned a given brand.
  • Limits. Single run per question, so brands at very low share are noise, not trends. One engine, so results do not transfer across engines. A snapshot in time, since web results move.
